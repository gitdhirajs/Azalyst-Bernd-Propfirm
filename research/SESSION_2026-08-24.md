n = **219** `CampusValuationTool` legend frames carrying a parsable argument list, across

```

Conditioning on what is on screen splits it cleanly:

| **Daily** | 115 | 57 | 6 | 50 | `{10,30}` → **107/115 = 93%** |

## 3. DIRECTION CHAPTER — the trend rule, verbatim

`2024-03-06_Direction`, presented by **Chris Dietenberger** (frame_000001 title card).
Slide text, transcribed verbatim across several frames:

> **UP-TREND ANALYSIS** — Identify HL&HH **from current price!** Uptrend **Required: 2 x HL**
> **General Rule: Identify 6 recent pivots (3 lows and 3 highs) to establish current trend**

> Downtrend: 2x Lower Highs (LH) · Uptrend: 2x Higher Lows (HL) · Sideways: no clear trend is
> recognizable

> **SIDEWAYS** — Rule of thumb: if no clear trend is recognizable, then define it as sideways

> Trend analysis does not tell us when the market is likely to change its direction (Where is
> the end of the bend?) **Early trend anticipation > Trend analysis**

Our `_determine_trend()` (`BP_rules_engine.py:1005`) already matches this closely: it takes
the most recent 3 highs and 3 lows from ZigZag pivots and applies the **asymmetric** test
(higher lows ⇒ uptrend, lower highs ⇒ downtrend, else sideways). The existing code comment
cites "OTC Lesson 4 frames 378/466" for the asymmetry; this chapter is independent
corroboration from a different session and a different presenter.

**One spec-vs-code inconsistency, not yet measured.** The code uses
`all(ll_vals[i] > ll_vals[i-1])` over *whatever it has*. With 3 lows that demands 2 rising
steps — matching "2 x HL". With only 2 lows it demands 1 step, which is weaker than the
spec. So the strictness of the test depends on how many pivots ZigZag happened to find.
Whether that matters is **unmeasured**; do not "fix" it without an A/B.

Context for why this is worth chasing: `our_trend` is **sideways on 65 of 133 rows (49%)**,
and **all 6 short-side OPPOSITEs occur on sideways rows**:

```

### E-05 restated correctly

A distance gate **does** exist — `BP_rules_engine.py:660-674`, committed **5b4a0c3, 23 Jun
2026**, a month before the log window opens. It enforces *both* an R cap and a raw-% cap
(weekly 3R / 15%, monthly 5R / 25%) and rejects on either.

It is only evaluated **at signal creation**. `risk.pending_order_max_age_days` is
`{weekly: 14, daily: 5, monthly: 30, intraday: 2}`, and **nothing re-checks distance while an
order rests**. A limit placed 6% away can sit for a fortnight while the underlying walks away
from it.

Measured over all 72 pending rows Discord printed — **every one of them dated on/after
27 Jul 2026**, so this is current behaviour, not history:

```
median 7.76% away    max 31.46%    17 rows beyond the 15% weekly cap
CL=F  BUY-LIMIT  E:58.470   printed "29.04% away" on 4 consecutive scans
```

**The fix is to re-apply the distance cap in `check_pending_fills()`, not to add a gate that
already exists.** (One `(nan% away)` row on `CL=F` is the same un-root-caused `nan` price bug
the 08-23 note flagged on `^GDAXI`.)

### E-06 is fixed — measured

The correlation cap on pending→active promotion **and** the pending expiry both landed in
commit **69c7314, 27 Jul 2026, "fix: wire portfolio risk gates that existed but were never
called"** — three weeks *before* the 08-23 session read the log.

The 08-23 exposure figures (`EUR +23, AUD −14, GBP −11, NZD −10, JPY +10`) are counts over
**all 66 emitted signals across the whole log**. Emitted signals are not what the cap
governs — the cap runs at `add_position`. Measuring the quantity Rule #12 actually caps,
**concurrent net exposure within a single scan**:

| window | scans | max open positions in one scan | worst single-currency net |
|---|---|---|---|
| before 69c7314 | 61 | 6 | **EUR +4** (2026-07-22 03:51) |
| on/after 69c7314 | 49 | **2** | **1** (2026-08-15 08:47) |

The commit did what it says. **E-06 should be closed.**
