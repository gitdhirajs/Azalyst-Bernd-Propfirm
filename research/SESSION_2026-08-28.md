## 1. The previous session's conclusion was measured on a path that never trades

`ZoneDetector.rank_zones` has three call sites. Two rank **HTF** zones; one -- the trading
path, `BP_rules_engine.py:366` -- ranks **LTF** zones after `align_multi_timeframe`. LOL is
only ever written onto LTF zones, so on the HTF paths it is 0.00 by construction.

The 248-zone sample behind "LOL is dead" came from the HTF diagnostic fallback. On the
trading path LOL has mean 4.28, sd 3.22, and is the **least** saturated of the seven.

Probed properly (`goldtest/zone_qualifier_stats.py`, 13,482 zones), the real problem is the
opposite of the one recorded:

    departure       72.4% at 10.0      Q1 repeats the DETECTION GATE verbatim
    base_duration   77.8% at 10.0
    profit_margin   82.4% at 10.0      Q5 hardcoded to 10 when with_trend
    arrival         80.4% at 10.0      Q6 hardcoded to 10 when with_trend
    freshness       91.6% at 0.0
    level_on_top    26.4% at 2.0       <- the only one that varies freely

Q1 scores 10 if `body_pct >= 0.70`; a candle only becomes a leg-out if `body_pct >= 0.70`.
It carries the largest weight (0.30). Q5/Q6 award full marks on trend-aligned zones -- the
methodology says skip the *gate*, the code also destroys the *measurement*.

## 2. Q5 measures zone age, not profit margin (C-109)

`margin_distance` is the largest excursion since the zone formed, over **all loaded
history**. Older zone, bigger number. Measured values run 181 to 874 against a threshold
of 5. The 11% that score 0 are the *youngest* zones -- the ones nearest price, the ones you
could actually be filled at. Q5 systematically rewards ancient zones and penalises fresh ones.

## 3. The real defect: ranking has no term for where price is (C-110)

Against 65 of their own drawn setups, weekly strategy, median 93 candidates each:

    engine rank-1 zone      median 6.29R from their drawn entry
    best candidate in pool  median 0.11R
    rank-1 IS their zone    0 of 65
    their zone's rank       median 60 of 93
    rank-1 zone age         904 bars    theirs: 467 bars

Their zone is nearly always in the pool, 0.11R away. We rank it 60th and pick a zone from
years earlier that price will never revisit.

Ordering rules, all fit-free, split-half validated:

    current composite        0% hit@1     4.74R median error
    pseudo-random null       0%           5.91R
    nearest to price        20%           2.64R
    reachable 10% + composite 11%         2.02R

**The seven-qualifier composite does not beat a pseudo-random ordering of its own pool.**
Reachability held at every band (2/5/10/20%) and both halves -- 12 of 12 cells improved.

Every qualifier repair failed: Q1 on departure strength **worse**, Q5/Q6 on real
measurements **identical**, dropping Q5 **noise**, fewest-retests (last session's candidate)
**worse**, LOL alone **worse**.

Shipped behind `BP_ZONE_REACHABLE`, **default OFF**. Reachability is an ordering, never a
rejection, so it cannot empty the candidate list. Flag off with a price passed produces a
byte-identical order.

On the 510-case corpus: **signals 8 -> 55**, accuracy unchanged at 52.75%. That is the
mechanism confirmed -- rank-1 was unreachable, so no entry ever triggered.

## 4. The null was wrong all session (C-111)

`expectancy.py` compared everything against P(+2R before -1R) = 1/3, exact **on a driftless
series**. The C-110 arm is 33 of 34 long and 28 of 34 in 2023.

    drift-matched null (same symbols/directions, 1355 random entries)   41.0%   +0.23R
    driftless theory null quoted all session                            33.3%   +0.00R

The bar was 7.7 points too low. Under the correct null, neither the zone entries (22.7%,
p=0.061) nor the directions (53.3%, p=0.117, CI 35-71%) are significant. `--drift-null` added.

**Their published KPI slide (40.0% / +0.20R) is indistinguishable from the drift-matched
null (41.0% / +0.23R) on this symbol set and era.** Not a claim about their real trading --
different instruments, era, discretion, and a slide is not a track record. It does mean the
KPI cannot be cited as evidence of edge over "long these markets in 2023".

**The effect shrank every time the data got better.** Direction is consistent across every

## 6. Entry placement is a dead end; the zone is the lever (C-112)

The three arms lined up monotonically against their own nulls -- proximal -18.3 points,
midpoint -8.3, market +4.3 to +12.3 -- which looked like adverse selection on entry depth.
It was not. Those were three different signal populations (34 vs 28 trades).

Controlled: one signal set, entries re-priced across a depth sweep, stop held fixed.

    our zones     depth 0.00  22.7%  -19.6 vs null      their zones   39.5%   +4.4
                  depth 0.25  27.3%  -13.4                           34.2%   -0.3
                  depth 0.50  23.8%  -15.8                           27.0%   -6.6
                  depth 0.75   5.6%  -28.7                           27.8%   -2.5

No depth beats the null for our zones. The midpoint arm's better headline came from
emitting a different, smaller trade set -- not from the midpoint. Their entries are BEST
at depth 0 and degrade with depth, as a deliberately chosen level should.

**Their zones +4.4 points, ours -19.6, at the same depth against each sample's own null.**
A ~24-point gap that is entirely about WHICH ZONE gets selected. z=1.33, p=0.185 --
consistent in direction, underpowered, like everything else at this sample size.

Entry-placement work is now finished and negative: midpoint entry, depth tuning and C-104
are all measured and none is the lever.

## 7. Stage-1 accuracy is closed (C-113, C-114)

The user asked whether 80% accuracy matching their calls is achievable. Measured, not
estimated. A maximally overfit lookup table over the seven bias components -- memorise
the best answer for all 158 combinations -- caps at **71.4% in-sample**. 80% is not hard,
it is arithmetically unavailable by rule tuning.

Out-of-sample, forward splits by date, three independent feature families:

    zone-derived         62.4% in-sample   44.24% out   -9.41 vs baseline
    market state         68.4%             48.24%       -5.41
    engine indicators    68.6%             49.65%       -4.00
    ALL 15 FEATURES      96.5%             50.12%       -3.53

96.5% in-sample against 50.12% out is fitting noise. **575 subsets searched across all
families: best result anywhere -0.47 points.** Nothing beats a constant. The engine's own
additive-vote architecture scores -8.47.

On directional cases only (dropping the 41% neutral class), "always long" already scores
**85.67%** -- above the 80% target, today, with 0-12% short recall and zero information.

So the 80% question resolves as: unreachable on the full metric, trivially available and
meaningless on the directional one. Twenty phases of `_bias_consensus` tuning did not fail
from bad design; there was no signal in those inputs to find.
